The board
is the vision

WE ARE DESTINY 3 is an open community exploration of what Destiny could become. Not a leak. Not a prediction of what Bungie is building. A blank canvas with a lot of people drawing on it.

Destiny has one of the most recognizable visual and gameplay identities in games. The interesting question isn't how faithfully you can reproduce it — it's where it goes next. What replaces the ability HUD. What a destination feels like when it couldn't have existed on last-generation hardware. What happens to Supers, to weapons, to fireteams, to the Tower.

There is no single WE ARE DESTINY 3 vision, and there is no committee assembling one. The concepts people post are the vision, and it changes every time someone adds to it.

One idea, one entry

This is the rule the whole site is built around, and it's what keeps the board from turning into a feed of disconnected images.

If you make five videos exploring the same combat scenario, that is one concept with five explorations — not five entries. The board shows the idea. The explorations live inside it as evidence, annotated for what worked and what didn't.

That distinction matters most for generative work, where no single output is ever quite right. One generation has the best HUD, another has the best weapon effect, a third accidentally discovers something nobody was aiming for. Presented as five separate posts, that reads as noise. Presented as one concept with the good seconds pulled out of each, it reads as design research — which is what it actually is.

Explorations, not versions

They're numbered but not ranked. "v3" implies it beats v2. In practice exploration 01 might have the only usable HUD in the set while exploration 04 has the only usable lighting. Each one is labelled by what it was testing, not by how far along it is.

Status, not quality

Every concept carries one of three states, so the board shows things evolving rather than arriving finished:

  • Exploring — many directions open, nothing settled.
  • Direction — a clear idea has emerged from the noise.
  • Refined — a polished representation of the concept exists.

Starting at Exploring is normal. Most good ideas never leave it, and posting something unsettled is the point rather than a compromise.

Made with anything

Concept art, Blender renders, Unreal scenes, Photoshop, Figma prototypes, handwritten sketches, AI images, AI video, UI mockups, design documents, music, sound design, animation, lore, weapon designs, playable experiments. All of it belongs here.

The standard is interesting direction, not how it was made. Arguments about whether someone's tools count are off-topic — take them elsewhere.

How it runs

GitHub is the collaboration layer; this site is the showcase. Concepts are submitted as GitHub Issues or pull requests, stored as one folder per concept in the repository, and published here automatically when they land. Media is hosted by GitHub — attachments for normal files, Releases for large ones.

Everything is public, including the argument about what should be in it.

Credit

Every concept carries its creator's name, and creators keep the rights to their own work. Building on someone else's concept is encouraged — remix their HUD, animate their weapon, score their trailer — as long as you credit and link them.

Evolve, don't reskin

Explain the thinking

A good concept isn't only beautiful. Tell us what problem you're solving or what feeling you're chasing.

Keep the failures

The generation that only got one thing right still got one thing right. Annotate it and keep it.

Remix each other

One person's HUD becomes another person's gameplay video. Credit the original and build on it.
